# Canonical filtered root bundle on an ordered packet collision face

## Status

This note globalizes the pointwise root-function filtration of the coalescing packet Jacobian over the ordered nondegenerate common-scale collision face.

The result is intrinsic to the realization map and the chosen collision stratum:

- the filtration does not depend on a reference-pair chart;
- it does not depend on the Hermite or Smith frame used to compute it;
- its associated graded pieces are holomorphic vector bundles;
- every positive packet exponent contributes a canonical holomorphic line bundle;
- the missing exponent is the global identity
  \[
  \mathcal F^m=\mathcal F^{m+1},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{gr}^m\mathcal F=0.
  \]

The individual Hermite-dual root germs remain choices of local lifts. Their classes provide local frames of the canonical graded line bundles.

No unordered quotient is asserted here. The base is the ordered common-scale collision face with pairwise distinct normalized shape, nonzero limiting coefficients, and separated exterior nodes.

---

## 1. Ordered collision face and source bundle

Let

\[
\mathcal R_N(X,u)
=
\left(\sum_{j=1}^N u_jx_j^k\right)_{k=0}^{2N-1}
\]

be the square finite moment realization.

Fix a cluster size \(m\ge2\). Let

\[
\mathcal B_m^{\mathrm{ord}}
\]

denote the ordered nondegenerate common-scale collision face. Locally, a reference-pair chart \(\alpha=(a,b)\) has coordinates

\[
(h,p_\alpha),
\]

where \(h\) is the boundary defining function and \(p_\alpha\) collects the collision center, normalized shape, coefficients, and exterior packet data.

On an overlap between charts \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), write

\[
(h_\beta,p_\beta)
=
\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}(h_\alpha,p_\alpha),
\]

and let

\[
Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}
:=
D\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}
\]

be the ordinary source-coordinate transition matrix. The scale functions satisfy

\[
\boxed{
 h_\beta
 =
 \lambda_{\alpha}^{\beta}(h_\alpha,p_\alpha)h_\alpha,
}
\]

where \(\lambda_{\alpha}^{\beta}\) is holomorphic and nowhere zero on the overlap. For reference-pair charts,

\[
\lambda_{ab}^{cd}(0,p)=\xi_d-\xi_c.
\]

The restrictions

\[
Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}(0,p)
\]

are the transition functions of the ordinary source tangent bundle restricted to the boundary. Denote this rank-\(2N\) bundle by

\[
\mathcal E
:=
T\widetilde{\mathcal P}_N\big|_{\mathcal B_m^{\mathrm{ord}}},
\]

where \(\widetilde{\mathcal P}_N\) denotes the ordered common-scale blow-up region under consideration.

In chart \(\alpha\), let

\[
J_\alpha(h,p)
=
D\mathcal R_{N,\alpha}(h,p)
\]

be the realization Jacobian in the corresponding source coordinates. The chain rule gives

\[
\boxed{
J_\alpha
=
J_\beta Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}.
}
\]

## 2. Intrinsic root filtration in one chart

For an integer \(q\ge0\) and a boundary point \(p\), define

\[
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^q
:=
\left\{
 v_0:
 \begin{array}{l}
 v(h)\text{ is a holomorphic source-vector germ},\\
 v(0)=v_0,\\
 J_\alpha(h,p)v(h)=O(h^q)
 \end{array}
\right\}.
\]

Set

\[
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^0=\mathcal E_{\alpha,p}.
\]

The spaces are nested:

\[
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^{q+1}
\subseteq
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^{q}.
\]

### Proposition 2.1 - independence of the boundary defining function

Suppose

\[
\widehat h=\gamma(h,p)h,
\qquad
\gamma(0,p)\ne0.
\]

Then

\[
J(h,p)v(h)=O(h^q)
\]

if and only if

\[
J(h,p)v(h)=O(\widehat h^q).
\]

### Proof

Because \(\gamma\) is a holomorphic unit,

\[
\widehat h^q=\gamma(h,p)^qh^q.
\]

Multiplication or division by \(\gamma^q\) does not change membership in the principal ideal generated by \(h^q\). Hence the two order conditions are equivalent. \(\square\)

### Proposition 2.2 - first filtration term

At every boundary point,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^1
=
\ker J_\alpha(0,p).
}
\]

### Proof

If \(v_0\in\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^1\), evaluate

\[
J_\alpha(h,p)v(h)=O(h)
\]

at \(h=0\) to obtain

\[
J_\alpha(0,p)v_0=0.
\]

Conversely, if \(J_\alpha(0,p)v_0=0\), use the constant germ \(v(h)=v_0\). Holomorphy gives

\[
J_\alpha(h,p)v_0
=
J_\alpha(0,p)v_0+O(h)
=
O(h).
\]

Thus the two spaces agree. \(\square\)

## 3. Local Smith description

On every nondegenerate ordered chart, the proved varying-shape normal form is

\[
\boxed{
J_\alpha(h,p)
=
A_\alpha(h,p)D_m(h)B_\alpha(h,p),
}
\]

where \(A_\alpha,B_\alpha\) and their inverses are jointly holomorphic, and

\[
D_m(h)
=
\operatorname{diag}
\left(h^{\alpha_1},\ldots,h^{\alpha_{2N}}\right)
\]

with partial multiplicities

\[
\boxed{
(\alpha_i)
=
\left(
\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{2N-2m+2},
1,2,\ldots,m-1,
m+1,\ldots,2m-1
\right).
}
\]

For \(q\ge0\), define the fixed coordinate subspace

\[
E^{\ge q}
:=
\operatorname{span}\{e_i:\alpha_i\ge q\}.
\]

### Proposition 3.1 - local formula

For every boundary point in the chart,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^q
=
B_\alpha(0,p)^{-1}E^{\ge q}.
}
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
v(h)=B_\alpha(h,p)^{-1}w(h).
\]

Since \(A_\alpha\) is holomorphically invertible,

\[
J_\alpha v=O(h^q)
\]

is equivalent to

\[
D_m(h)w(h)=O(h^q).
\]

For coordinate \(i\), this condition is

\[
h^{\alpha_i}w_i(h)=O(h^q).
\]

If \(\alpha_i<q\), then \(w_i(h)\) must vanish to order at least \(q-\alpha_i\), so \(w_i(0)=0\). If \(\alpha_i\ge q\), the value \(w_i(0)\) is arbitrary. Therefore

\[
w(0)\in E^{\ge q},
\]

and applying \(B_\alpha(0,p)^{-1}\) proves the formula. \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 - constant rank

The rank is independent of the boundary point:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal F^q
=
\#\{i:\alpha_i\ge q\}.
}
\]

Explicitly, for \(q\ge1\),

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal F^q
=
\begin{cases}
2m-q-1,
&1\le q\le m-1,\\
m-1,
&q=m,m+1,\\
2m-q,
&m+1\le q\le2m-1,\\
0,
&q\ge2m.
\end{cases}
\]

The overlap at \(q=m+1\) is intentional; both displayed formulas give \(m-1\).

### Corollary 3.3 - local holomorphic subbundles

For every \(q\), the family

\[
p\longmapsto\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^q
\]

is a holomorphic subbundle of the local source bundle.

### Proof

The columns

\[
B_\alpha(0,p)^{-1}e_i,
\qquad
\alpha_i\ge q,
\]

form a holomorphic frame and remain linearly independent because \(B_\alpha(0,p)\) is invertible. \(\square\)

## 4. Chart invariance

### Theorem 4.1 - intrinsic overlap law

On every overlap between ordered collision charts \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\),

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}(0,p)
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^q
=
\mathcal F_{\beta,\Phi_{\alpha}^{\beta}(p)}^q.
}
\]

### Proof

Take

\[
v_0\in\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^q.
\]

Choose a holomorphic germ \(v_\alpha(h_\alpha)\) with

\[
v_\alpha(0)=v_0,
\qquad
J_\alpha(h_\alpha,p)v_\alpha(h_\alpha)=O(h_\alpha^q).
\]

Transport it to the \(\beta\)-coordinates by

\[
v_\beta
:=
Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}v_\alpha.
\]

The chain-rule identity gives

\[
J_\beta v_\beta
=
J_\beta Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}v_\alpha
=
J_\alpha v_\alpha
=
O(h_\alpha^q).
\]

Since

\[
h_\beta
=
\lambda_{\alpha}^{\beta}h_\alpha
\]

with \(\lambda_{\alpha}^{\beta}\) a unit, Proposition 2.1 converts this to

\[
J_\beta v_\beta=O(h_\beta^q).
\]

At the boundary,

\[
v_\beta(0)
=
Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}(0,p)v_0.
\]

Hence

\[
Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}(0,p)
\mathcal F_{\alpha,p}^q
\subseteq
\mathcal F_{\beta,\Phi(p)}^q.
\]

Applying the same argument to the inverse chart transition gives the reverse inclusion. \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 - global filtered root bundle

For every \(q\ge0\), the local bundles \(\mathcal F_\alpha^q\) glue to a globally defined holomorphic subbundle

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F^q\subseteq\mathcal E
}
\]

over \(\mathcal B_m^{\mathrm{ord}}\), and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E=\mathcal F^0
\supseteq
\mathcal F^1
\supseteq
\cdots
\supseteq
\mathcal F^{2m-1}
\supseteq
\mathcal F^{2m}=0.
}
\]

The filtration is intrinsic to the realization germ along the ordered collision face.

### Corollary 4.3 - independence of Smith and Hermite choices

Any two local analytic Smith factorizations, or any two Hermite-adapted constructions producing such factorizations, define the same subbundles \(\mathcal F^q\).

### Proof

The definition of \(\mathcal F^q\) uses only the order condition

\[
Jv=O(h^q).
\]

The Smith formula computes this intrinsic space but does not define it. \(\square\)

## 5. Associated-graded bundles

Define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_q
:=
\operatorname{gr}^q\mathcal F
=
\mathcal F^q/\mathcal F^{q+1}.
}
\]

Because \(\mathcal F^{q+1}\) is a holomorphic subbundle of \(\mathcal F^q\) of constant rank, \(\mathcal L_q\) is a holomorphic vector bundle.

### Theorem 5.1 - graded ranks

For every \(q\ge0\),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal L_q
=
\#\{i:\alpha_i=q\}.
}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal L_0
=
2N-2m+2,
}
\]

and for positive orders,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal L_q
=
\begin{cases}
1,
&q\in\{1,\ldots,m-1,m+1,\ldots,2m-1\},\\
0,
&q=m,\\
0,
&q\ge2m.
\end{cases}
}
\]

### Proof

In a Smith chart,

\[
\mathcal F^q
=
B(0,p)^{-1}E^{\ge q},
\qquad
\mathcal F^{q+1}
=
B(0,p)^{-1}E^{\ge q+1}.
\]

The quotient is therefore locally isomorphic to

\[
E^{\ge q}/E^{\ge q+1},
\]

whose dimension is the number of coordinates with \(\alpha_i=q\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 - global missing exponent

On the full ordered nondegenerate collision face,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F^m
=
\mathcal F^{m+1},
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_m
=
\operatorname{gr}^m\mathcal F
=0.
}
\]

Thus the missing packet exponent is not only a pointwise Smith invariant. It is a global vanishing statement for the associated-graded root bundle.

## 6. Hermite roots as local frames of canonical lines

Let

\[
E_m^+
:=
\{1,\ldots,m-1,m+1,\ldots,2m-1\}.
\]

For each \(q\in E_m^+\), let \(i(q)\) be the unique Smith coordinate satisfying

\[
\alpha_{i(q)}=q.
\]

In a chart \(\alpha\), define the Hermite-Smith root germ

\[
r_{\alpha,q}(h,p)
:=
B_\alpha(h,p)^{-1}e_{i(q)}.
\]

Its boundary value lies in \(\mathcal F_\alpha^q\), and its class

\[
\boxed{
 s_{\alpha,q}(p)
 :=
 [r_{\alpha,q}(0,p)]
 \in
 \mathcal L_{q,p}
}
\]

is nonzero.

### Proposition 6.1 - local line frames

For every \(q\in E_m^+\),

\[
s_{\alpha,q}
\]

is a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic local frame of the line bundle \(\mathcal L_q\).

### Proof

The vector \(r_{\alpha,q}(0,p)\) lies in \(\mathcal F^q\). Its Smith coordinate has exponent exactly \(q\), so it does not lie in \(\mathcal F^{q+1}\). Hence its quotient class is nonzero. Holomorphic dependence follows from that of \(B_\alpha^{-1}\). \(\square\)

### Proposition 6.2 - scalar overlap cocycle

On an overlap, there is a unique nowhere-vanishing holomorphic function

\[
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{(q)}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
[Q_{\alpha}^{\beta}(0,p)r_{\alpha,q}(0,p)]
=
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{(q)}(p)
[r_{\beta,q}(0,\Phi(p))]
}
\]

in \(\mathcal L_q\).

The functions satisfy the line-bundle cocycle identities

\[
\boxed{
\gamma_{\alpha\gamma}^{(q)}
=
\gamma_{\beta\gamma}^{(q)}
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{(q)}
}
\]

on triple overlaps and

\[
\gamma_{\alpha\alpha}^{(q)}=1.
\]

### Proof

Theorem 4.1 shows that the transported class belongs to \(\mathcal L_q\). Both it and \(s_{\beta,q}\) are nonzero local sections of the same rank-one holomorphic bundle, so they differ by a unique holomorphic unit. The cocycle law follows from the cocycle law for the ordinary tangent transitions \(Q\). \(\square\)

### Interpretation

The line \(\mathcal L_q\) is canonical. The Hermite root is a distinguished local lift of that line. A different Smith or interpolation frame changes the lift by:

1. a nowhere-zero scalar on the graded line;
2. a section of the deeper filtration \(\mathcal F^{q+1}\);
3. higher normal Taylor terms.

## 7. Canonical associated graded object

The filtered ordinary source bundle has the intrinsic associated graded

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)
=
\bigoplus_{q=0}^{2m-1}\mathcal L_q.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)
=
\mathcal L_0
\oplus
\bigoplus_{q\in E_m^+}\mathcal L_q,
}
\]

because \(\mathcal L_m=0\).

Its rank is

\[
(2N-2m+2)+(2m-2)=2N.
\]

### Proposition 7.1 - no canonical splitting asserted

Every local Smith frame gives a local splitting

\[
\mathcal E
\cong
\mathcal L_0
\oplus
\bigoplus_{q\in E_m^+}\mathcal L_q,
\]

but the present results do not produce a canonical global splitting of the filtration.

The associated graded bundle is canonical; a splitting requires additional choices or structure.

### Relation to the rescaled tangent bundle

In a Smith chart, the rescaled frame is

\[
T_{\alpha,i}(h,p)
=
h^{-\alpha_i}r_{\alpha,i}(h,p).
\]

Thus each local rescaled vector is obtained by desingularizing a local lift of the corresponding graded root direction at its exact order.

This gives a local identification between the boundary fiber of the rescaled tangent bundle and the associated graded root bundle. The current theorem does not claim that these local identifications define a choice-free global splitting. What is global without further choices is:

1. the filtered ordinary source bundle \((\mathcal E,\mathcal F)\);
2. its associated graded bundle;
3. the separately established rescaled tangent bundle and its extended metric.

## 8. Main theorem

### Theorem 8.1 - canonical filtered packet-root bundle

Over the ordered nondegenerate common-scale \(m\)-cluster collision face \(\mathcal B_m^{\mathrm{ord}}\), the order-of-vanishing spaces

\[
\mathcal F_p^q
=
\left\{
 v(0):v(h)\text{ holomorphic and }J(h,p)v(h)=O(h^q)
\right\}
\]

define a chart-independent decreasing filtration by holomorphic subbundles

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E=\mathcal F^0
\supseteq
\mathcal F^1
\supseteq
\cdots
\supseteq
\mathcal F^{2m-1}
\supseteq
\mathcal F^{2m}=0.
}
\]

The rank of \(\mathcal F^q\) is the number of packet local Smith partial multiplicities at least \(q\). The associated-graded bundles satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{gr}^q\mathcal F
=
\#\{i:\alpha_i=q\}.
}
\]

Consequently:

1. \(\operatorname{gr}^0\mathcal F\) has rank \(2N-2m+2\);
2. every exponent
   \[
   q\in\{1,\ldots,m-1,m+1,\ldots,2m-1\}
   \]
   defines a canonical holomorphic line bundle
   \[
   \mathcal L_q=\operatorname{gr}^q\mathcal F;
   \]
3. the missing exponent is the global identity
   \[
   \boxed{
   \mathcal F^m=\mathcal F^{m+1},
   \qquad
   \mathcal L_m=0;
   }
   \]
4. the explicit Hermite-dual roots are nowhere-zero local frames of these canonical graded lines;
5. their scalar overlap functions form holomorphic line-bundle cocycles.

### Proof

The local Smith formula and Proposition 3.1 give holomorphic constant-rank local subbundles. Theorem 4.1 proves that ordinary source-coordinate transitions preserve the order filtration, because the Jacobians are related by the chain rule and the two boundary defining functions differ by a holomorphic unit. Therefore the local subbundles glue. Quotients of nested constant-rank holomorphic subbundles give the associated-graded bundles. Their ranks follow from the packet partial multiplicities. Simplicity of every positive partial multiplicity gives the line-bundle statement, while absence of the value \(m\) gives \(\mathcal F^m=\mathcal F^{m+1}\). Proposition 6.1 identifies the Hermite roots as local frames, and Proposition 6.2 gives their overlap cocycles. \(\square\)

## 9. Claim boundary and next step

This note proves the filtered and graded bundle structure only on the ordered, nondegenerate, common-scale collision face.

It does not yet prove:

- extension across further collisions in normalized shape space;
- compatibility between different cluster sizes or nested strata;
- permutation equivariance under the full symmetric group;
- descent to an unordered quotient;
- triviality or nontriviality of the graded line bundles;
- a canonical splitting of the filtration;
- a Lie-algebroid bracket.

The immediate next theorem is permutation equivariance:

\[
P_\sigma\mathcal F_p^q
=
\mathcal F_{\sigma p}^q,
\]

followed by the induced action on every graded line bundle and on the rescaled metric bundle.
